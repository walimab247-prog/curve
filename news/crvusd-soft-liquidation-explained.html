Understanding crvUSD and Its Soft-Liquidation Design

A look at how crvUSD's lending-liquidating AMM (LLAMMA) uses oracle prices to convert collateral gradually, and what that means for users and the wider stablecoin landscape.

Source(s):

  • Curve documentation — crvUSD
  • Curve governance forum

Published: 2026-07-14

Updated: 2026-08-10

Author: Curve Learn Editorial Team

What happened

Curve's native stablecoin, crvUSD, introduced a mechanism called LLAMMA — a lending-liquidating automated market maker. Instead of liquidating a borrower's collateral in a single event when its price crosses a threshold, LLAMMA converts collateral into crvUSD gradually across a price range, and can convert it back if the market recovers. The design depends on an external price reference: the system compares an oracle price with its internal state to decide how collateral should be rebalanced.

Why it matters

Traditional liquidation models can be harsh: a brief price wick may wipe out a position entirely. Soft liquidation spreads that process out, which in principle reduces the damage caused by short-lived volatility. It is also a notable example of oracle-aware protocol design, where the accuracy and manipulation-resistance of the price feed directly shape user outcomes.

Risks and limitations

Soft liquidation is not loss-free: while collateral is being rebalanced, borrowers experience continuous small losses, and in fast, sustained crashes positions can still be fully converted. The mechanism also inherits oracle risk — if the reference price is wrong or delayed, rebalancing decisions will be wrong too.

Market context

Decentralized stablecoins remain an active area of experimentation, and collateral-management design is one of the key ways projects differentiate themselves. Observers generally treat LLAMMA as an interesting engineering approach rather than a guarantee of stability; its behavior in extreme markets continues to be studied by researchers and risk teams.
