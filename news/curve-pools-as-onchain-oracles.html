Why Other Protocols Use Curve Pools as On-Chain Price Oracles

Deep-liquidity Curve pools publish smoothed internal prices that external protocols consume as on-chain oracles — a practice with clear benefits and specific failure modes.

Source(s):

  • Curve documentation — pool oracles
  • ethereum.org — Oracles

Published: 2026-06-30

Updated: 2026-08-02

Author: Curve Learn Editorial Team

What happened

Several Curve pool implementations expose an internal price value computed as an exponential moving average (EMA) of recent trades. Because these values live entirely on-chain and are smoothed over time, external lending markets and stablecoin systems have adopted them as price references, alongside or instead of externally operated oracle networks.

Why it matters

On-chain, protocol-native oracles remove a dependency on off-chain reporters: there is no committee of node operators to trust, only the pool's own trading activity. The EMA smoothing raises the cost of manipulation, since an attacker must move the pool price and keep it moved across multiple blocks rather than for a single transaction.

Risks and limitations

The approach works best for deep pools; a shallow pool's EMA can be shifted with relatively little capital. Smoothing also introduces lag — during genuine sharp moves, the EMA temporarily deviates from the live market price, which consuming protocols must account for. Historical DeFi incidents show that misjudging either factor can be costly.

Market context

The industry trend is toward combining multiple oracle types: externally aggregated feeds, on-chain TWAP/EMA references, and sanity bounds enforced by the consuming contract. Curve's pool oracles are one component in that layered picture rather than a universal replacement.
